## Dynamic branching and scoring
**Branch selection:** all player choices transition to the next authored stage, with preceding `effects` persisted and displayed in the business/operational ledger. The consequences are conditional on **which exact option was selected**; alternative histories cannot be inferred or invented by the model. At the next stage, show the earlier effect as a status banner, then the shared incoming inject. This is a deterministic **converging branch graph** (3 × 3 × 3 = 27 complete paths), not a linear quiz and not 27 separately scripted stories.

**Rule evaluation:** the only score changes are option `rules` plus valid board checklist rules; stage rules are looked up in the [editable catalog](../03_DECISION_RULES.md). Risk appetite affects only the four explicit modifier keys. **The later event does not retrospectively change S1 scoring.** The `effects` fields are fictional teaching levers, not independent scores or real-world loss forecasts.

**Regulatory context:** [REG-NIS2-21](../04_REGULATORY_REGISTER.md#reg-nis2-21), [REG-NIST-CSF-GV](../04_REGULATORY_REGISTER.md#reg-nist-csf-gv). The rules do not establish legal applicability by citation alone. Any duty and timeline must be assessed on a separate applicability/trigger panel; when unavailable show `PENDING_CLASSIFICATION`. No notification-omission rule is enabled in this scenario without future validated source facts.

## Board room, defense and seven-part pack
**Chair asks:** “Why cannot CIO and CISO agree on one delivery recommendation?”

**Three structured challenges:** (1) CEO asks what happened and what is still unknown; (2) CFO asks what the decision costs versus alternatives; (3) Chair asks for a specific resolution, named accountable owner and reassessment checkpoint. Board persona affects only tone, never factual claims or score.

**Mandatory pack:** 1. Situation: summarize currently established facts and timestamp. 2. Business impact: quantify authored ledger and confidence. 3. Risk: material cyber/operational exposure and unknowns. 4. Recommendation: selected action and rejected alternative. 5. Investment: explicitly authorized/required budget; distinguish fictional figures. 6. Residual risk: engine status, owner and next control point. 7. Board resolution: one unambiguous approval/acceptance/action request.

**Sample management line (coaching, not score):** “Cross-functional influence and executive maturity. My recommendation is conditional on the next evidence checkpoint; I request that the board agree the limit of authorized exposure and the accountable owner.”

## Learning objectives, failure modes and evidence
- **Objectives:** Conflict negotiation, Operational constraints, Governance.
- **Failure modes:** excessive certainty from missing evidence; treating a framework citation as an automatic legal duty; undocumented risk acceptance; unbounded business disruption; mistaking scenario consequence for an assessment penalty; reporting as if all legal clocks shared a trigger.
