## Executive simulation script

**Situation (visible at start).** A commercially influential vice president has bypassed security controls to launch a new client platform, and audit logs confirm it. At stake — whether the rules bind everyone, what the client relationship is really worth and how the next exception is decided.

**Stakeholder pressures.** CEO: Protect the client relationship. Legal: Be consistent and document every exception.

| Stage | Evidence/pressure inject | Decision and trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Authorise the response to the confirmed bypass | **A:** Contain the exposure and run the same documented corrective process that applies to anyone / **B:** Grant an informal exception because of who the executive is / **C:** Shut down every customer platform while the investigation runs |
| S2 | Day 2 — The vice president threatens to move the clients to a competitor unless the restriction is lifted. Revenue is material. What do you authorise? | **A:** Offer time-boxed compensated controls and put the decision to the CEO as the accountable owner / **B:** Keep the exception off the board record / **C:** Name and blame the individual publicly before any review is done |
| S3 | Day 5 — The board asks whether the rules apply to commercially important executives. Whatever you answer becomes the standing rule. What do you tell them? | **A:** Show one exception standard for everyone and how precedents will be reviewed / **B:** Argue that the policies are optional for revenue-generating leadership / **C:** Promise that a breach like this can never happen again |

## Dynamic branching and scoring
**Branch selection:** all player choices transition to the next authored stage, with preceding `effects` persisted and displayed in the business/operational ledger. The consequences are conditional on **which exact option was selected**; alternative histories cannot be inferred or invented by the model. At the next stage, show the earlier effect as a status banner, then the shared incoming inject. This is a deterministic **converging branch graph** (3 × 3 × 3 = 27 complete paths), not a linear quiz and not 27 separately scripted stories.

**Rule evaluation:** the only score changes are option `rules` plus valid board checklist rules; stage rules are looked up in the [editable catalog](../03_DECISION_RULES.md). Risk appetite affects only the four explicit modifier keys. **The later event does not retrospectively change S1 scoring.** The `effects` fields are fictional teaching levers, not independent scores or real-world loss forecasts.

**Regulatory context:** [REG-DORA-5](../04_REGULATORY_REGISTER.md#reg-dora-5), [REG-GDPR-32](../04_REGULATORY_REGISTER.md#reg-gdpr-32). The rules do not establish legal applicability by citation alone. Any duty and timeline must be assessed on a separate applicability/trigger panel; when unavailable show `PENDING_CLASSIFICATION`. No notification-omission rule is enabled in this scenario without future validated source facts.

## Board room, defense and seven-part pack
**Chair asks:** “Do security policies apply to commercially important executives?”

**Three structured challenges:** (1) CEO asks what happened and what is still unknown; (2) CFO asks what the decision costs versus alternatives; (3) Chair asks for a specific resolution, named accountable owner and reassessment checkpoint. Board persona affects only tone, never factual claims or score.

**Mandatory pack:** 1. Situation: summarize currently established facts and timestamp. 2. Business impact: quantify authored ledger and confidence. 3. Risk: material cyber/operational exposure and unknowns. 4. Recommendation: selected action and rejected alternative. 5. Investment: explicitly authorized/required budget; distinguish fictional figures. 6. Residual risk: engine status, owner and next control point. 7. Board resolution: one unambiguous approval/acceptance/action request.

**Sample management line (coaching, not score):** “Consistency, legitimate exceptions and governance. My recommendation is conditional on the next evidence checkpoint; I request that the board agree the limit of authorized exposure and the accountable owner.”

## Learning objectives, failure modes and evidence
- **Objectives:** Policy equity, Exception lifecycle, Organizational culture.
- **Failure modes:** excessive certainty from missing evidence; treating a framework citation as an automatic legal duty; undocumented risk acceptance; unbounded business disruption; mistaking scenario consequence for an assessment penalty; reporting as if all legal clocks shared a trigger.
